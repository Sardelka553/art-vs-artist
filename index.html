Art vs. Artist · research study
Undergraduate research · University of the People

Can you separate the art from the artist?

Almost every month, someone who made something you love turns out to have done something you can't ignore. This study asks a narrower, testable version of that question: when you know what a creator did, does the show itself actually feel different?

Psychologists have already shown this effect for paintings. Nobody has tested it for television, where a show is made by hundreds of people and a good story pulls you in. That's the gap this study is trying to close — and it needs your ratings to do it.

What you'll actually do

  • Answer three quick questions about yourself — no name, no email, nothing identifying.
  • Watch three short clips from real television shows, roughly 1–4 minutes each.
  • Rate each one on five sliders and say, in your own words, what you think.
  • See the live results dashboard — everyone's answers so far, updated the moment you submit.

About 7–10 minutes. Best with sound on.

One thing you should know now: participants are randomly split into two groups that read slightly different background information before each clip. You'll be told exactly which group you were in, and why, on the final page. Nothing you read here is fabricated — every factual claim is sourced and linked.

Your privacy

  • No personal data is collected. No name, email, IP address, login, or tracking cookie.
  • You may enter a made-up nickname if you'd like to spot your own row later. Leave it blank if you prefer — it's optional, and you should not use your real name.
  • Age is recorded as a broad band and location as a world region, so that no combination of answers can identify you.
  • Your answers are stored in a single spreadsheet owned by the student researcher and used only for this coursework.
  • You can stop at any point by closing the tab, and nothing is saved unless you press submit at the end.
Content note. The background information in this study describes real allegations of workplace abuse, sexual harassment, harassment of minors, and racism. It is written factually and without graphic detail, but if that's not something you want to read today, please don't take part.

By pressing the button below you confirm you are 18 or over, that you're taking part voluntarily, and that you agree to your anonymous answers being used in this student research project.
